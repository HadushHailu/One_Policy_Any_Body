Task Conditioning & Temporal Ensemble

Multi-task disambiguation and smooth action generation

Horizon Clarity

Tasks need names — without an explicit signal, multi-task policies collapse to the average of incompatible behaviors.

The Mode Collapse Problem

Consider training a single policy on two tasks: pick & place (move object to zone A) and stack (place object on top of another). After grasping, the policy must decide: move to zone A, or to the other cube?

Why averaging destroys performance

Let $a_\text{pick}$ = move-to-zone-A and $a_\text{stack}$ = move-to-other-cube. Without task signal:

$$\pi(a \mid o) = \frac{1}{2}\pi_\text{pick}(a \mid o) + \frac{1}{2}\pi_\text{stack}(a \mid o)$$

A regression-based policy outputs the expected action:

$$\mathbb{E}[a] = \frac{a_\text{pick} + a_\text{stack}}{2}$$

This midpoint action moves to neither target — between zone A and the other cube. 0% success on both tasks.

"But diffusion handles multimodality!" — true, it picks one mode per rollout. But which mode? It's random. You get 50% pick, 50% stack, regardless of what the user wants. That's not controllable multi-task learning.

The Task Embedding

The fix is simple: give the policy an explicit task ID $k \in \{0, 1, \ldots, K-1\}$ and learn a task-specific embedding:

Task encoder

$$\text{task\_emb}(k) = E_k \in \mathbb{R}^{32}$$

where $E \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times 32}$ is a learnable embedding matrix (just like word embeddings in NLP).

class TaskEncoder(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, n_tasks=2, embed_dim=32):
        super().__init__()
        self.embedding = nn.Embedding(n_tasks, embed_dim)

    def forward(self, task_id):
        # task_id: (B,) long tensor
        return self.embedding(task_id)  # (B, 32)

The embedding is learned — the network discovers what "pick" and "stack" mean in terms of the action space differences. No manual encoding needed.

Combined Conditioning Vector

The full conditioning for the U-Net combines morphology and task:

Conditioning architecture

$$\text{morph\_cond} = [\text{morph\_encoder}(m) \;\|\; \text{task\_encoder}(k)] \in \mathbb{R}^{64}$$

This 64-dim vector enters FiLM at every U-Net layer:

$$\text{FiLM}(h, \text{morph\_cond}) = \gamma(\text{morph\_cond}) \odot h + \beta(\text{morph\_cond})$$

The full conditioning stack:

$$\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, o, m, k) = \text{UNet}\big(x_t,\; \underbrace{\text{timestep}(t) + \text{obs}(o)}_{\text{global cond}},\; \underbrace{[\text{morph}(m) \| \text{task}(k)]}_{\text{FiLM cond}}\big)$$

Information flow

Observation tells the policy what it sees.
Morphology tells it what body it has.
Task tells it what to do.
Together, they uniquely determine the action.

The Chunk Boundary Problem

Recall receding-horizon control: execute $k=8$ actions, then re-plan. At the boundary between chunks, the last action of chunk $n$ and the first action of chunk $n+1$ are generated by different forward passes. They can be inconsistent — causing jitter.

Symptom: The robot's end-effector makes small jerky adjustments every 8 timesteps. Visible as periodic "hiccups" in otherwise smooth trajectories.

Temporal Ensemble — The Mathematics

The solution: when chunks overlap in time, average their predictions with exponential decay weighting.

Ensemble formulation

At timestep $t$, multiple chunks may have predictions. Chunk $i$ started at step $s_i$ and predicted:

$$a_t^{(i)} = \text{chunk}_i[t - s_i]$$

The weight for chunk $i$'s prediction at time $t$:

$$w_i = \exp(-\lambda \cdot (t - s_i))$$

where $\lambda$ is the decay rate. Lower $\lambda$ = more smoothing.

The ensembled action:

$$a_t = \frac{\sum_i w_i \cdot a_t^{(i)}}{\sum_i w_i}$$

Intuition

Recent predictions (low $t - s_i$) get high weight because they were conditioned on more recent observations. Old predictions (high $t - s_i$) fade away — they were based on stale information.

Decay Rate Analysis

$\lambda$BehaviorUse case
0Equal weight (simple averaging)Maximum smoothing
0.01Gentle decay over ~100 stepsTypical for 10Hz control
0.1Fast decay, recent chunks dominateDynamic environments
1.0Only latest chunk mattersEquivalent to no ensemble

We use $\lambda = 0.01$ — smooth enough to eliminate jitter but responsive enough to track changing targets.
