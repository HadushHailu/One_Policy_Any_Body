## 1. Theoretical Foundations for Week 3

### T1: Domain Randomization for Robust Policies

#### T1.1 Why Randomize During Collection?

Until now, all 5 demos per robot used **fixed** physics and object positions. A policy trained on these would fail if:
- The cube spawns 2cm to the left
- Friction is slightly different
- The camera shifts by a pixel

**Domain randomization (DR)** creates diverse training data by varying:

| Parameter | Nominal | Randomization Range | Why |
|----------|---------|--------------------|-----|
| Cube position | (0.4, 0.0, 0.02) | ±5cm XY | Different grasp positions |
| Cube mass | 0.05 kg | ±30% | Variable inertia |
| Table friction | μ=1.0 | ±30% | Different sliding behavior |
| Joint damping | nominal | ±20% | Real motors aren't perfect |
| Camera position | fixed | ±2cm | Calibration errors |
| Lighting intensity | 1.0 | [0.5, 1.5] | Real-world lighting varies |
| Table color | gray | random RGB | Visual diversity |

#### T1.2 Which Parameters Matter Most?

From sim-to-real literature (Tobin et al., 2017; Peng et al., 2018):

1. **Object pose randomization** — MOST IMPORTANT. Without this, the policy only works at one position.
2. **Visual randomization** — Critical for image-based policies (ours uses ResNet-18 on images).
3. **Physics randomization** — Helps but less critical for position-controlled robots.

#### T1.3 Implementation: Already in Codebase

`opab/env/domain_randomization.py` has `DomainRandomizer` with `DRConfig`. Currently disabled (`enabled=False`). This week we:
1. Set `enabled=True` in demo collection
2. Add cube position randomization to the env reset (±5cm XY)
3. Verify demos still succeed >80% with DR active

### T2: Multi-Task Learning — Shared vs Separate Representations

#### T2.1 The Multi-Robot Learning Challenge

When training on data from *multiple robots*, the network must learn:
- **What's shared:** Task structure (approach → grasp → lift → move → place)
- **What's robot-specific:** Reach, speed, gripper width, joint dynamics

Without morphology conditioning (baseline B2), the model must infer the robot from images alone — ambiguous when cameras are positioned similarly.

With OPAB's FiLM conditioning, the morphology vector **explicitly tells** the U-Net which robot it's generating actions for. The denoiser can:
- Share low-level motion primitives across robots
- Adapt speed/amplitude via FiLM scale (γ)
- Shift action offsets via FiLM bias (β)

#### T2.2 Data Mixing Strategy

| Strategy | Pros | Cons | Our Choice |
|----------|------|------|:---:|
| Sequential (robot A then B) | Simple | Catastrophic forgetting | ✗ |
| **Interleaved (shuffle all)** | No forgetting, balanced | Simple | **✓** |
| Curriculum (easy → hard) | Can help convergence | Complex to implement | Later |
| Per-robot dataloaders | Ensures balance | More complex | Fallback |

Our `MultiRobotDataset` already shuffles across all robots — each batch naturally contains a mix of Franka, UR5, and SO-101 samples.

#### T2.3 Expected Behavior

- **Loss should be slightly higher** than single-robot training (more diversity)
- **Convergence should be slower** (more modes in the action distribution)
- **Final performance on each robot** should be comparable to single-robot training
- **Key graph:** Plot loss curves for: single-franka, single-ur5, multi-robot

### T3: Data Scaling Laws for Imitation Learning

#### T3.1 How Many Demos Do We Need?

Empirical scaling for diffusion policy BC (from Chi et al., 2023):

| Demos | Expected Behaviour |
|------:|:---|
| 5 | Memorization — works only for seen positions |
| 10 | Slight generalization to nearby positions |
| 25 | Begins to generalize across workspace (>30% success) |
| 50 | Solid performance (>50%) with some failure modes |
| **100** | **Strong performance (>70%)** — our target per task/robot |
| 200+ | Diminishing returns for scripted data |

**Our plan:** 100 demos per robot × 2 tasks = 200 episodes per robot. Total 600 across 3 robots. This should be sufficient for >70% success on trained embodiments.

#### T3.2 Why Not More?

- Each 100-episode collection takes ~10 min (scripted policy at MuJoCo speed)
- Disk: 100 eps × 300 steps × (84×84×3 + 4 + 7) ≈ 6 GB per task/robot
- With 3 robots × 2 tasks = ~36 GB total (fits on disk, fits in RAM if subset)
- Diminishing returns: 200 demos isn't 2× better than 100

#### T3.3 Data Efficiency Is Our Story

If OPAB achieves 70% with 100 Franka + 100 UR5 demos and **transfers to SO-101 zero-shot**, that's the paper. If it needs 10 SO-101 demos to reach 70%, that's still a strong story (10× data reduction vs training from scratch).

### T4: Cross-Embodiment Action Alignment

#### T4.1 Why Task-Space Actions Enable Transfer

Our action space is `[dx, dy, dz, gripper]` — delta end-effector position in world frame + gripper command. This is **already robot-agnostic**:

```
Same command: action = [0.01, 0.0, -0.005, 0.0]

Franka: IK → 7 joint deltas → ee moves 1cm forward, 0.5cm down
UR5:    IK → 6 joint deltas → ee moves 1cm forward, 0.5cm down
SO-101: IK → 5 joint deltas → ee moves 1cm forward, 0.5cm down
```

The IK solver handles the robot-specific mapping. The policy never needs to know about joints.

#### T4.2 Workspace Normalization

Different robots have different workspace volumes:

| Robot | Workspace Radius | Table Height | Cube XY Position |
|-------|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| Franka | 0.855m | 0.0m | (0.4, 0.0) |
| UR5 | 0.85m | 0.2m | (0.45, 0.0) |
| SO-101 | 0.30m | 0.0m | (0.15, 0.0) |

**Problem:** Even though actions are in world frame, the *scale* of actions differs:
- Franka approach: move 0.3m down to reach cube
- SO-101 approach: move 0.1m down (smaller workspace)

**Solution:** The action normalizer (trained on all robots) will learn robot-specific scales. Combined with the morphology vector (which encodes workspace_radius), the policy learns to output appropriately-scaled actions for each robot.

### T5: Episode Filtering — Quality over Quantity

#### T5.1 Why Filter?

Not all scripted demonstrations succeed. We filter to keep only high-quality episodes:

| Filter Criterion | Threshold | Reason |
|-----------------|-----------|--------|
| Success | `True` | Only keep successful grasp-and-place |
| Steps | ≥50, ≤300 | Too short = physics glitch, too long = stalling |
| Cube moved | >5cm | Verify the cube actually went somewhere |
| Gripper closed during grasp | Check gripper_pos < 0.5 | Verify actual grasping occurred |

#### T5.2 Target Success Rates with DR

| Robot | Without DR | With DR (expected) | After Filtering |
|-------|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| Franka | 100% | ~85% | Collect 120, keep 100 |
| UR5 | 80% | ~70% | Collect 145, keep 100 |
| SO-101 | 100% | ~80% | Collect 125, keep 100 |

We'll over-collect and filter to exactly 100 successful episodes per task/robot.

### T6: Task Diversity for Generalization

#### T6.1 Why Add Stacking?

Pick-and-place alone teaches:
- Approach, grasp, lift, move, place

Stacking (place cube A on cube B) additionally teaches:
- Precise height targeting
- Orientation awareness (can't place off-center)
- Sequential planning (approach cube A before cube B)

This additional task structure helps the policy learn more reusable manipulation primitives.

#### T6.2 Stacking Task Design

```
Scene: table + cube_A (red, random position) + cube_B (blue, random position)
Goal: place cube_A on top of cube_B

Scripted Policy States:
  APPROACH_A → GRASP_A → LIFT_A → MOVE_OVER_B → PLACE_ON_B → RELEASE → RETREAT

Success: cube_A z-position > cube_B z-position + cube_size - tolerance
```

#### T6.3 Pouring Task (Optional/Stretch)

Pouring is harder (continuous control, orientation) and may not work with scripted policies. **Deprioritized** — only attempt if stacking is working smoothly by Wednesday.

## 5. Troubleshooting Guide

### Problem: Scripted policy success rate drops below 50% with DR
**Cause:** Object position randomization is too large for the robot's workspace.
**Fix:** Reduce `cube_pos_range` from ±5cm to ±3cm. For SO-101 (small workspace), use ±2cm.

### Problem: Out of RAM loading 600 episodes
**Cause:** 600 eps × 300 steps × 84×84×3 = ~30 GB images alone.
**Fix:** 
1. Load images lazily (only read from HDF5 on `__getitem__`)
2. Or compress images in memory (keep as uint8, convert to float in __getitem__)
3. Or subsample episodes (use 100 from each dataset)

### Problem: Multi-robot training loss is much higher than single-robot
**Cause:** Action scale differences between robots (SO-101 actions are 3× smaller).
**Fix:** Normalize actions per-robot (compute stats per robot, not globally). The normalizer already does per-dim normalization — verify it handles the multi-robot case correctly.

### Problem: Multi-robot training favours one robot over others
**Cause:** Unbalanced dataset — if Franka has 100 eps with 300 steps and SO-101 has 100 eps with 200 steps, Franka has 50% more samples.
**Fix:** Weight sampling by robot (use `WeightedRandomSampler`) or truncate all episodes to same length.

### Problem: Stacking policy keeps missing the target cube
**Cause:** The "place on top" height offset is wrong.
**Fix:** Target = `cube_b_pos + [0, 0, cube_size * 2 + margin]`. MuJoCo measures from geom center, so top of cube_b is at `cube_b_z + cube_size`.

### Problem: DR causes visually unrealistic scenes
**Cause:** Table color randomization produces impossible colors.
**Fix:** Constrain to realistic table colors (wood/gray/white range) instead of full RGB.
